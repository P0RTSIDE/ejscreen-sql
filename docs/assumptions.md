# Assumptions for the EJScreen version 2.3 reconstruction

Formulas follow EPA, *EJScreen Technical Documentation for Version 2.3*, revised July 31, 2024, and no other page, where those sources disagree. Local copy: `docs/sources/ejscreen-tech-doc-version-2-3.pdf`.

## Unemployment versus persons with disabilities

Decision: the fifth-factor set in version 2.3 includes percent persons with disabilities. It does not include unemployment. The supplemental demographic index is the average of z-scores, then shifted so the smallest value becomes zero. This follows the version 2.3 tech doc only.

Exact version 2.3 sentence, p. 33:

> The five socioeconomic indicators considered are low life expectancy, percent low income, percent persons with disabilities, percent limited English speaking, and percent less than high school education.

Page 31 lists the same five factors as z-score inputs, in a different order: percent low income, percent limited English speaking, percent less than high school education, percent persons with disabilities, and low life expectancy. Unemployment remains a standalone socioeconomic indicator, from ACS table B23025, and is not in that average (pp. 6 to 10).

Other EPA pages say unemployment, and some of them average the raw percents instead of the z-scores.

- EPA news release "EPA Launches Updates to Environmental Justice Mapping Tool EJScreen," published October 11, 2022, describing EJScreen 2.1: "The five socioeconomic indicators considered are percent low-income, percent limited English-speaking, percent less than high school education, percent unemployed, and low life expectancy." The same release says the EJ indexes "use an average of percent low income and percent people of color." URL opened: `https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/epa-launches-updates-environmental-justice-mapping-tool-ejscreen`.
- *EJScreen Technical Document Appendix*, October 2022, for version 2.1. Printed page 2 lists Unemployment Rate, ACS 2020, as a supplemental factor. Printed page 4 gives `Supplemental Demographic Index = (% Low Life Expectancy + % Low Income + % Unemployment Rate + % Limited English Speaking + % Less Than High School Education) / 5`, and a four-factor average that still includes unemployment when life expectancy is missing. URL opened: `https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2023-01/Technical-Documentation-Appendix-for-2.1.pdf`.
- EPA EJScreen Change Log, captured as a 23-page PDF on December 11, 2024, and read from Harvard Dataverse file id 10776235 in dataset `10.7910/DVN/RLR5AX`. Under July 2024 changes, which include "Release of EJScreen 2.3," PDF page 4 says the supplemental index was reformulated to include disabilities and exclude unemployment. It prints the old calculation as the five percents with unemployment, divided by 5, and the new calculation as `(% Low Life Expectancy + % Low Income + % Disability + % Limited English Speaking + % Less Than High School Education) / 5`. The next bullet says the Demographic Index and Supplemental Demographic Index "were changed to using z scores."

The change log therefore states both a percent average that includes disability and a switch to z-scores. The version 2.3 tech doc states the z-score average, the four-factor fallback when life expectancy is missing, and the non-negative shift (pp. 31 to 35). The reconstruction uses that tech doc text. It does not use the change log's percent-average line as the formula.

## Other places EPA pages disagree

These are recorded and not resolved by picking a side other than the version 2.3 tech doc, where a formula is at stake. Where the tech doc itself prints two statements, both are left standing.

1. Percentile ties. Tech doc pp. 36, 40, and 42: floor method. If a value of 0 maps to percentiles 0 through 10, report 0, not 10. Change log PDF page 2, "August 2024 Data Patch" to version 2.3 data: a value between two cutoff values still goes to the lower percentile, but if that percentile is the last in a run of tied cutoffs, the final percentile is the last of the tied values, which the change log describes as rounding that last tied cutoff up rather than down. The tech doc PDF was produced September 18, 2024, and modified September 19, 2024, and the body still prints the floor example. The printed revision date remains July 31, 2024. Formula authority stays with the tech doc floor example.

2. EJ index cap. Version 2.1 appendix, printed page 2, says the multiplication was set "so that the maximum EJ Index cannot exceed 100." Version 2.3 pp. 36 to 39 print the multiplication and a worked product of 49.532, and they do not state a cap. Tech doc silent on a cap. Pages checked: 36 to 39.

3. What gets multiplied. Version 2.3 p. 36 says the Demographic Index in the EJ index equation is the average of the two z-scores. Page 33 says a shift is added after that average. The worked example on p. 37 multiplies the shifted value (0.8395), not the unshifted average (-0.501221). The supplemental example on pp. 38 to 39 does the same. 5. Low-income boundary. Prose on p. 8: income less than or equal to twice the poverty level. Formula on pp. 8 to 9 and p. 32: `(C17002.001 - C17002.008) / C17002.001`, and C17002.008 is the category "2.00 and over." The tech doc does not discuss people whose ratio is exactly 2.00.

7. Population versus block groups. Page 40: the 80th percentile nationwide "means 20% of the U.S. population has a higher value." ## Sample standard deviation

The tech doc defines `z = (x - mu) / sigma` and does not say whether sigma divides by n or by n - 1 (pp. 31 to 33). The worked example on pp. 36 to 38 prints standard deviations. Those printed figures match the sample standard deviation, divisor n - 1, of the block groups that have data. They do not match the population divisor n. Two comparisons from that example decide it. Low income is printed as standard deviation 0.213371, and percent persons with disabilities as 0.062492. The reconstruction uses the sample standard deviation. Mu is the unweighted average of block groups with a non null value for that indicator. National mu, sigma, and the shift use the 50 states, the District of Columbia, and Puerto Rico. State mu, sigma, and the shift are computed inside each state, and the same way for the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, and each of the four island territories. The shift adds the absolute value of the smallest unshifted index in that geography, which is how pp. 33 and 35 describe the step that makes the index non negative. Where the smallest value is negative, adding its absolute value is the same operation as subtracting that smallest value.

## Percentile cutoffs

The tech doc stores a raw cutoff at each integer percentile from 0 through 100 and then assigns the lower end of the range a value falls in. Ties use the floor (pp. 40 to 42). The tech doc does not name the interpolation that places those 101 cutoffs. This reconstruction sets cutoff p with SQL `percentile_cont` at fraction p / 100, for p = 0, 1, ..., 100. That is linear interpolation at position `1 + (n - 1) * (p / 100)`. A value is assigned the highest percentile whose cutoff is less than or equal to the value. If the value equals that cutoff and several percentiles share it, the value takes the lowest of those tied percentiles. A raw 0 that is the cutoff for percentiles 0 through 10 is percentile 0. Null indicator values are excluded. National percentiles exclude the four island territories. State percentiles partition by state. The primary percentile is this cutoff lookup.

## State indexes

The tech doc does not print a separate multiplication equation for state EJ indexes (pp. 36 to 37 and 41 to 43). State demographic indexes use the state mean, the state sample standard deviation, and the state shift. A state EJ index multiplies the shifted state demographic index by the state environmental percentile on a 0 to 100 scale. A state supplemental index multiplies the shifted state supplemental demographic index by that same state environmental percentile. The national file stores the state demographic indexes as `DEMOGIDX_2ST` and `DEMOGIDX_5ST`. The state extract stores those state indexes under `DEMOGIDX_2` and `DEMOGIDX_5`, and stores state percentiles and state index values under the `P_`, `D2_`, and `D5_` names.

## Validation tolerances, fixed before the score comparison

Continuous rebuilt values, the demographic indexes and the EJ and supplemental indexes, count as inside tolerance when the absolute difference is at most 1e-8. The reference stores these fields at 15 decimal places, so the comparison does not apply the rounded figures printed in the tech doc. 1e-8 is above ordinary double precision residue for values of this magnitude, and below the change from a one point percentile error or from using the population standard deviation. Percentile columns are integers. Their tolerance is 0, so the tolerance match is the exact match. A column passes the exact check when at least 99 percent of rows with both values present are equal as double precision numbers. A column passes the tolerance check at the same 99 percent bar. Null agreement passes when at least 99.9 percent of rows agree on whether the value is null. If both sides are null on every row, the exact check is treated as passed and the null check carries the comparison. A low match rate is a reported fail. It does not stop the build.

The build stops if an assertion fails. The assertions are: unique ids, a 12 character geoid, population null or at least 0, percentiles inside 0 to 100 and whole numbered, a shifted demographic index at least 0, the minimum shifted index at 0 in each geography that has values, and an EJ or supplemental index equal to the product of the shifted demographic index and the environmental percentile. The tech doc states no maximum of 100 for that product (pp. 36 to 39). The assertion does not add one.
